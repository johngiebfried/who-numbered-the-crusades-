# French diffusion, 1842-1914: what was reached (task 5)

13 November 2026. 15 rows in `entries_fr.csv`, 21 searches logged. This is a partial result: the school series and the programmes are mostly not reached, and no share can be stated.

## 1. Result

Among the reference works actually read, the modern eight is the norm from 1842 on. Of six enumerating reference rows (Bouillet 1842 and 1863, Dezobry 1863, Larousse GDU 1869, Grande encyclopédie c. 1892, Petit Larousse 1906), five have it and one does not. The exception is the largest French dictionary of the century, Larousse's GDU (1869), which has its own eight. Larousse itself changes by 1906: the Petit Larousse says "Les Croisades sont au nombre de huit" and lists 1202-04 fourth, Andrew II/Damietta fifth, Frederick II sixth. The Nouveau Larousse illustré (1897-1904), the step in between, was not reached.

Two negative findings matter for the title question. The Académie's dictionary never numbers beyond "première" in the 6th (1835), 7th (1878) or 8th (1932-35) editions, and the 8th has no ordinal at all. The stock example "la huitième et dernière croisade" is therefore not in the 8th edition; it must enter with the 9th, not fetched. And the Duruy school history I could read (Histoire de France, t. 1, Hachette 1876) does not run to eight: it numbers "la quatrième croisade" (Villehardouin) but gives Louis IX "deux croisades," the second being the "dernière croisade" of 1270.

The panel is not a closed census yet, so the claim, restricted to what was read: "The modern eight is attested in five of six enumerating French reference rows of 1842-1906; the exception is Larousse's GDU of 1869. Absent in N=0 works before 1842 in this task (Strand A and B cover that)." No share is stated: nine panel works were reached of a panel not yet listed.

## 3. First edition with the modern eight, per long-running work (attested, not "first")

- **Bouillet:** 1842 (title page seen; Strand A). Same scheme in the printing dated 1863 (title page not seen); the only change is the fifth, 1218 in 1842 and 1217 in 1863. Editions 1857 to 1908 not read otherwise.
- **Dezobry-Bachelet:** the 1863 printing; the 1857 first edition not read.
- **Grande encyclopédie:** t. 13 (c. 1892, year recalled), independent wording and a named author (A. Mounier per OCR); numbered section headings Quatrième to Huitième croisade.
- **Petit Larousse:** the 1906 file; the 1905 first edition not reached.
- **Larousse GDU:** never, in the t. 5 of 1869.
- **Duruy, Histoire du moyen âge:** the French text was not reached. The English translation of the 12th edition (Holt 1891) has it: contents "Fourth Crusade (1201-1204)" and "Seventh and Eight Crusades (1248 and 1270)", fifth = Andrew II, sixth = Frederick II in the body. The reported 1861 modern eight is unverified. The translators' revisions could have changed the numbering.

## 4. Schemes that change between editions or products

- Larousse: GDU 1869 (larousse_eight) to Petit Larousse 1906 (modern eight). Two different products of one house; the change falls inside the unread Nouveau Larousse illustré, the Larousse supplements, or the first Petit Larousse.
- Bouillet: no change of scheme between 1842 and the 1863 printing, only a date.
- Académie: no scheme, no change.

## 5. Programmes and Catholic series

Not reached. The programmes are printed in INRP, *L'histoire et la géographie dans l'enseignement secondaire: textes officiels*, t. 1: 1795-1914 (2000), where the arrêté of 28 January 1890 is item 150 (Persée gives metadata only). The 1865 programme is dated 25 March in a footnote of a secondary source. Dates for 1852, 1880 and 1902 stay recalled. Ducoudray (Hachette, "conforme aux programmes de 1882") and Gagnol (Catholic, classe de cinquième) are at Gallica texteBrut URLs that returned an error. The confession test has no data. A priority for the author: the programmes will say whether the Université's lists named any crusade, but the schoolbooks read so far (Duruy 1876, Langlois 1901, Lavisse 1913) do not enumerate, so the school route to the eight is not supported by anything read here.

## 6. What this does and does not support

It does not support the assumption that the school system made the eight standard by way of Duruy's histories; it does show reference works (Bouillet, Dezobry, the Grande encyclopédie, Petit Larousse) with the eight from 1842 and one large dictionary without it in 1869. Whether schools ever counted to eight in the Third Republic is unknown from this task.
