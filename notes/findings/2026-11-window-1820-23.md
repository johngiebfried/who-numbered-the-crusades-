# The 1820–23 window and the verification pass

27 November 2026. One session, browser only: Gallica (SRU, ContentSearch, ALTO), the Google Books text layer and page images, the BnF and SUDOC catalogs, archive.org full text, and MDZ (IIIF images and hOCR). It covers directions A and B of the startup prompt. New rows: 2 in `data/strand_b/entries.csv` and 1 in `data/strand_b/entries_de.csv`. Ten rows were updated, 4 not-reached rows and 30 search-log rows were added. The script is `scripts/add_window_2026_11_27.py`.

## Result

As far as its printed texts can be read online, the Université did not prescribe the modern eight. Its official programme of 1820 counts seven. Its baccalauréat question table was ordered in March 1821 and is first read here in its 1826 state. It asks for a "précis historique des croisades" and numbers none. The eight first appears in the book Desmichels wrote for the new Middle Ages course and examination. That book was registered on 21 December 1822 with a title page dated 1823. One official text in the window is still unread: the 1822 printing of the history questions (BnF G-31078), which is probably the table's first printing (section A2). Until someone reads it, the answer is probable, not firm.

## A. The window

**The 1820 outline was an official programme.** The *Tableau sommaire du cours d'histoire générale* (Gallica bpt6k65448935) is headed "Commission d'instruction publique." View 4 says it "a été rédigé par MM. Desmichels et Auguste Trognon, professeurs d'histoire aux colléges royaux de Henri IV et Louis-le-Grand." The avertissement explains that the course was broader than "aucun des trois autres," so the authors "ont cru devoir excéder les limites d'un simple programme." The *Bibliographie de la France* registered it in April 1820 as no. 1350. Nos. 1351 and 1352 follow it: "Programme du cours de l'Histoire de France dans les collèges royaux" and "Programme pour l'enseignement de l'histoire ancienne dans les collèges royaux." All three have the same printer (Fain), the same publisher (Colas) and the same format (in-4). Boismilon's *Tables synchroniques à l'usage des cours d'histoire ancienne et moderne* appeared the same year in the same format (Colas, in-4, 36 p.; *Journal des savants* 1820, p. 761). The "histoire générale" course runs from 395 into the eighteenth century. Its chapter XIV, "De l'Orient et des croisades," was re-read for this session. It numbers the 1202 crusade fourth, Frederick II fifth, and Louis IX sixth and seventh. It names Andrew II's expedition without a number and closes with "Résultats généraux des croisades." **Firm.**

**The 1821 examination table split off the Middle Ages and did not number the crusades.** Hubert's *Manuel des lois et règlemens sur les études … de la médecine* (Paris: Gabon, 1826; Google Books JBI7AAAAcAAJ) prints the baccalauréat ès lettres question table, because medical students needed the bachelor's degree. A note on p. 3 refers the drawing of questions by lot to the "Règlement du 13 mars 1821, art. 6." Series 2 covers history and geography. Its second section, "Histoire du Moyen Age," has 25 questions, and "Histoire moderne" begins at no. 26. Question 14 (p. 16, page image viewed) reads: "État de l'empire d'Orient et de l'Asie, à la fin du XIe siècle. — Précis historique des croisades. — Résultats généraux des guerres saintes. — Progrès de la navigation, du commerce, de la civilisation. — Ordres religieux et militaires. — Chevalerie." The topics are those of the 1820 programme's chapter XIV, but without its ordinals. The same wording heads the answers in Ponelle (1826, read; 1829), Delavigne (1829, 1835) and Bouchitté (1836); the last four were seen only in search snippets. The history questions were printed separately as *Questions sur l'histoire ancienne, l'histoire romaine, l'histoire du moyen âge, l'histoire moderne*. The publisher was Brunot-Labbe, "libraire de l'Université," and the printer was Fain. The *Bibliographie de la France* registered it on 15 June 1822 (no. 2839) and again on 28 December 1822 (no. 5792). The BnF holds the 1822 printing (44 p., G-31078) and an 1823 printing (48 p., 16-G-434). Neither is digitized. **Firm for 1826, provisional for 1821–22.**

**The answer books show where the numbers came from.** The 1824 *Manuel complet des aspirans au baccalauréat* (Delalain; both title pages now viewed) answers the questions in their numbered order. Its "§ XIV. Des croisades" copies Desmichels's numbered list. Ponelle's *Nouveau manuel* (1826; Gallica bpt6k97585500) answers question XIV with a narrative that numbers nothing but the fourth, and that only in passing ("lors de la quatrième croisade"). The examination asked for the crusades; the cram books decided whether to number them.

**The switch: 21 December 1822.** Desmichels's *Tableau chronologique de l'histoire du moyen âge* appears in the *Bibliographie de la France*, N° 51, Saturday 21 December 1822, as no. 5716 (in-8, 11¼ sheets, printed by Fain, published by Louis Colas). The issue header (Gallica bpt6k64262646, f753) and the entry (f764) were both read, and no other header falls between them. The 1823 date on the title page is therefore a post-date. Larousse's "Paris, 1822" is the year of registration, not a separate printing. The BnF has only the 1823-dated record (G-22668; Arsenal 8-NF-44836). The title page carries no "autorisé par le Conseil royal" line. The *Revue encyclopédique* reviewed the book in 1823 (no. 142, p. 359, signed "P."). The review lists the Colas tables of Poirson and Caix, du Rozoir, Trognon and Desmichels, and Boismilon as works "que l'Université … a commandées." It says Desmichels's volume "complète cette collection." It says nothing about the crusade numbers. **Firm.**

**The October 1822 revision is lost for now.** On 26 October 1822 the *Bibliographie de la France* registered a "Tableau sommaire pour servir à l'enseignement de l'histoire moderne; par MM. A. Trognon et Desmichels … Ouvrage autorisé par le conseil royal de l'instruction publique. Seconde édition, revue, corrigée et augmentée" (no. 4729; in-8, 4½ sheets). It is the second edition of the 1820 programme, retitled for "histoire moderne." Its smaller size and the *Revue encyclopédique*'s description both suggest that it dropped the Middle Ages, which Desmichels's separate table then covered. I found no copy in the BnF catalogue, in SUDOC or among digitized books.

**What Desmichels changed.** Between his 1820 programme and his 1822 table, Desmichels numbered Andrew's expedition fifth and moved Frederick II from fifth to sixth. Louis IX's first crusade went from sixth to seventh, and Tunis from seventh to "huitième et dernière." The result is Koch's 1771 slots with the French dictionaries' numbered eighth. No document found gives his reason.

**Reading (inference, flagged).** In 1821 the Université made the Middle Ages a separate examination subject. It then took up a series of course tables by Paris collège teachers, and Desmichels wrote the medieval volume. The examination question kept the 1820 topics and dropped the numbers. The answer books put the numbers back from Desmichels's table. The eight therefore reached students through the textbook and the cram manual, not through the examination text. Only the 1822 question pamphlet can overturn this. If its question 14 numbers the crusades, the switch belongs to the Université.

**Searched without result.** The *Almanach de l'Université* for 1822 lists Trognon at Louis-le-Grand but has nothing on numbering. Boismilon's tables are not digitized. Gallica has no Colas history volume of 1821–22 beyond the 1820 programme. (The statute of 4 September 1821 and the *Recueil de lois et règlemens*, first listed here as not found, turned up in the follow-up below.)

## A2. Follow-up, same day: the three leads

**Marchand's *Textes officiels* (Persée, read page by page).** Philippe Marchand, ed., *L'histoire et la géographie dans l'enseignement secondaire: textes officiels*, t. 1, *1795–1914* (INRP, 2000), prints five texts from the window. The arrêté of 9 November 1818 (text 17, p. 112) gives seconde the "histoire générale" from the invasions to the present, which is the course the 1820 programme served. The circular of 4 July 1820 (text 20, pp. 115–117; from R.L.R. t. 6, pp. 348–352) sends the rectors four programme cahiers: ancienne, romaine, générale and France, in-4, from Colas. That matches the *Bibliographie de la France* group of April 1820 (nos. 1350–52 and the Roman cahier). It hopes the programmes will receive "les développements dont ils ont besoin pour devenir des livres élémentaires." Desmichels's 1823 *Tableau chronologique* is exactly such a development. The statute of 13 September 1820 (text 21) puts history into the baccalauréat. The statute of 4 September 1821 (text 23, pp. 118–120; R.L.R. t. 7, pp. 119–124) gives troisième "l'histoire du Moyen Âge" (art. 172) and seconde "l'histoire moderne proprement dite" (art. 176). That split is the reason for both the separate medieval *Tableau chronologique* and the retitled October 1822 *Tableau sommaire … histoire moderne*. The règlement of 16 September 1826 (text 24) reorganizes the course again and falls outside the window. Marchand does not print the baccalauréat question table, and he does not mention Desmichels. His introduction (pp. 29–31) says he found only the Roman cahier (du Rozoir and Ragon, *Programme du cours d'histoire romaine*, BnF J-6398); the histoire générale cahier, which this project reads on Gallica, escaped him. **Firm** for the curriculum dates.

**The *Recueil de lois et règlemens*, t. 7 (1824), is on Google Books in full view** (--Ek8YYtTQ4C; other copies 5qzeAAAAMAAJ, 4wNSAAAAcAAJ). It prints the règlement of 13 March 1821 (pp. ~65–67, text read). Art. 6 orders that a table "en trois séries, des questions principales" *be drawn up* ("On rédigera à cet effet un tableau"); series 2 is history and geography, and the questions are drawn by numbered balls. The règlement does not print the table, and a search of the volume for "croisades" and "tableau des questions" finds no table elsewhere. Art. 1 moves the examination to the candidate's académie "à dater du 1er octobre 1822." A note points to the ordonnance of 17 October 1821 and the arrêté of 15 January 1822 (the latter on admission formalities only, p. 178).

**What this does to G-31078.** The règlement of March 1821 orders a question table but does not contain one. The first known separate printing of the history questions is Brunot-Labbe's *Questions*, sold by the "libraire de l'Université" and registered on 15 June 1822. It is therefore likely the first printing of the official table, issued for the new regime of autumn 1822 (inference, flagged). Hubert's 1826 text is a later state of the same table. G-31078 is no longer one witness among several. It is probably the official table itself, and it rises in weight accordingly.

**The 1822 *Tableau sommaire … histoire moderne*: still no copy.** Searched: Google Books (three hits, all references: the Colas advertisement in Desmichels 1823, the *Bibliographie de la France* 1822, and Misan 1985, p. 27, which lists it in Trognon's bibliography as "Paris, Colas 1822"); the BnF catalogue général (SRU: Trognon as author, 36 records; "tableau sommaire" with Colas as publisher, 1820 only); SUDOC (1820 only, PPN 098106228); the KVK (BVB, K10plus, SWB, Jisc Library Hub, BNE: no hits; BnF and BL failed through the KVK). Not reached: the CCFr federated search returned zero even for the 1820 edition, so the scripted search is not trusted; HathiTrust stopped at a bot check; the Emmanuelle database at inrp.fr did not load; WorldCat was not searched (terms gate). Two by-products of the BnF search: Trognon's *Discours prononcé pour l'ouverture du second semestre du cours d'histoire moderne* (18 April 1822; cb314979812) confirms that he taught the modern course in 1822, and Michelet's *Tableau chronologique de l'histoire moderne* (Colas, 1825; cb30943334m) shows that the modern volume of the Colas series later passed to Michelet.

**What this does to the German argument.** Four German rows carried the claim that German schoolbooks counted seven. Two have the full seven: Fiedler 1834 in the base form and the *Handbuch* 1835 in the merged form. The other two, Uschold 1839 and Weber 1847–49, stop at five. The claim survives in a narrower form. German school usage of the 1830s and 1840s fixed Frederick II as the fifth, and numbered Louis IX's crusades less consistently. The *Handbuch* is a Catholic book, so the seven was not confined to Protestant schools, which bears on direction E1 (the Netherlands). Five other rows coded in the family are still snippets: Streit 1842, Haas 1844, Beitelrock 1853, Dietsch 1854 and Kurts 1860. Beitelrock has only L1 6, which does not separate `frederick_fifth_seven` from `gibbon_seven`, so its family code should be cleared or checked.
