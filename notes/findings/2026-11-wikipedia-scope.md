# What counts as a crusade on Wikipedia: English and German

9 November 2026. Pilot for two questions, English and German only: (A) how the crusade side box has defined the field since 2004, and (B) how the unnumbered expeditions are named and described. Method and data at the end.

## Short answer

The two boxes went opposite ways. German drew a narrow, chronological line in 2005 and has barely touched it: 16 links then, 22 now, no sections, nothing north of the Elbe or south of the Pyrenees. English started wider than the numbered series and kept widening, from 12 links in 2004 to 107 in April 2025, then cut back to 64. Its fights are about scope, not numbers: the Reconquista has been in and out five times, early modern wars three times, popular crusades twice.

On names, the answer is negative and firm. Neither English nor German Wikipedia has ever given Henry VI's crusade, the Crusade of 1101, the People's Crusade, the Children's Crusade or any late crusade a number, in a title, a redirect, an opening paragraph or a Wikidata alias. The print tradition's Fourth for 1197 left no trace. The numbers on Wikipedia stayed attached to the canonical eight or nine; everything else got a date, a leader or a place.

Today's Holy Land section has 19 entries. Eight are numbered (First to Eighth). Eleven are not: 1101, Norwegian, Venetian, 1129, Rögnvald Kali's, 1197, Barons', 1267, Catalan, Lord Edward's, Fall of Outremer. In the Levant, the unnumbered expeditions now outnumber the numbered ones.

### German: Vorlage:Navigationsleiste Kreuzzüge (22 revisions)

Created 5 May 2005 by Bender235 as "Vorlage:Kreuzzüge", renamed August 2006. From the start it had the People's Crusade, "Deutscher Kreuzzug" (then the article on the 1096 pogroms), 1101, the Wendish and Albigensian crusades, the Children's Crusade, Alexandria and Nicopolis, all in one chronological line with the numbered crusades.

Additions since: Shepherds' Crusade 2007; Henry VI's crusade May 2008 (Herrgott, replacing "Deutscher Kreuzzug"); Edward's and the Barons' crusade October 2010 (Johnny47); Shepherds' 1320 in 2016; Norwegian 2023; Crusade of the Poor January 2026. A 2009 talk-page request for the Baltic, Swedish, Hussite and Iberian crusades ("entweder alle Kreuzzüge … oder nur die wichtigsten") was never acted on.

## B. Names and descriptions

- **Henry VI, 1197.** English created it as "Henry's crusade" (September 2007) and renamed it "Crusade of 1197" the same day ("better title"). Its aliases are Crusade of Henry VI, German Crusade, and "Emperor's Crusade", by analogy with the Third as the "Kings' Crusade". German: "Kreuzzug Heinrichs VI. … auch bekannt als Deutscher Kreuzzug" (2008). Every "Fourth Crusade" in either article (176 English revisions, 87 German) is the sentence saying that 1197 weakened Byzantium before 1204.
- **"Deutscher Kreuzzug"** meant the 1096 pogroms in German until 2008, and since 2018 is a disambiguation page listing 1096, Barbarossa's army and 1197. In English, "German Crusade, 1096" was dropped in 2008 for "no such usage in scholarship", followed by five years of renames ending at "Rhineland massacres".
- **1101.** English began as a redirect to the First Crusade (April 2004) and became an article in December 2004: a "minor crusade", the "Crusade of the Faint-Hearted", "in the aftermath of the First Crusade". German follows the same framing. No revision calls it the Second.
- **People's Crusade.** English: "part of the First Crusade" (2005), now its "initial phase". German: "Vorläufer des Ersten Kreuzzugs". The two editions disagree on whether it belongs to the First.
- **Mahdia, 1390.** English: "Siege of Mahdia" → "Mahdian Crusade" (2010, "systemically better to have it listed as a crusade") → "Barbary Crusade" (2015). German renamed "Barbareskenkreuzzug" to "Kreuzzug gegen Mahdia" (2010) because that is the name in the scholarship.
- **Nicopolis** is a battle article in both. English calls it "one of the last major Crusades". German called it "manchmal auch als der letzte Kreuzzug" from 2005 to at least 2011.
- **German's foreign count again.** "Hirtenkreuzzug von 1251" happened during the Sixth Crusade, "beziehungsweise nach englischer und französischer Zählung während des siebten". This matches the labeling already found in the Seventh and Edward articles.
- **A second numbered series.** English titles the Swedish expeditions First, Second and Third Swedish Crusade, and redirects "First Northern Crusade" to the Wendish Crusade. Wikipedia numbers in the north what it refuses to number in the Levant.

## Method

MediaWiki API through the browser, 9 November 2026. A: every revision of each box; all links extracted with their display label and section heading; renamed titles resolved to current articles; blanking vandalism dropped; gaps under three days merged. B: creation, renames, redirects, the opening paragraph sampled each 1 July, a full-revision search of the 1197 and 1101 articles for numbered names, Wikidata labels and aliases. The tables are not yet saved as files; the extraction reruns in a few minutes.
