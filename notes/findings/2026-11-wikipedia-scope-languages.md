# What counts as a crusade, language by language

Running list, started 9 November 2026. Crusade side boxes in 22 Wikipedias (25 templates), every revision since 2004, links mapped to Wikidata so the same expedition can be compared across languages. Data: `data/wikipedia/scope/box_membership_matrix.tsv` (one row per expedition, one column per language, the year it entered the box; `x25` = removed in 2025). English and German are written up in `2026-11-wikipedia-scope.md`.

## The three questions

**Spain and the Reconquista: no.** Spanish Wikipedia is not the edition that makes the Reconquista a crusade. Its box (2009) added the Reconquista only in 2012, and only as a single link; apart from the Balearic expedition of 1113–15 (2009–23), it has never listed an Iberian campaign one by one. The editions that do are Catalan (Majorca and the Pisan-Catalan expedition since 2010; a section "Contra taifes d'Al-Àndalus" since August 2026), English (Barbastro, Las Navas, Granada 2018–21) and Persian (2018–25). Portuguese has a Reconquista section since 2018 but leaves out Lisbon 1147; the only box with the siege of Lisbon is French.

**Scandinavia and the Baltic: yes, but not as expected.** The Danish box began in 2009 with the Teutonic Order and the Albigensian crusade, added the Sword Brothers, the Livonian Order and the Swedish and Wendish crusades in 2010, and got the numbered crusades only in 2013. Swedish puts "De svenska korstågen" beside the First to Third. Finnish went the other way: three "crusades to Finland" were added in 2005 and removed in 2007 ("otan raa'asti pois nuo kolme ristiretkeä Suomeen", "I am ruthlessly removing those three crusades to Finland"), leaving one general link. Meanwhile the same three expeditions, as First, Second and Third Swedish Crusade, sit in the English, Spanish, Italian, French, Arabic, Persian and Ukrainian boxes. The north numbers its crusades less than the rest of Wikipedia does.

**Arabic and Persian leaving out Europe's internal crusades: no.** Arabic added the Albigensian crusade ("the Cathars") and the Hussite Wars in 2017, the Aragonese crusade in 2019. Persian had the Hussites and Cathars within a week of creating its box (March 2016) and in 2025 added the Guelphs and Ghibellines, the League of Cognac and the Spanish Armada, which English had just removed. Persian now keeps three political crusades English has dropped. The narrow editions are Hebrew and Polish.

## Language by language

**Arabic.** Two boxes. The older (2004) was eight-only until 2013, widened in 2017 to the northern and heretic crusades; the Reconquista entered in 2018 as a section titled "سقوط الأندلس" (the Fall of al-Andalus, 718–1492). An attempt to copy the full English layout (Holy Leagues, Armada, Cambrai) in November 2024 was reverted the next day. The second box (2012) adds battles and 44 people, Muslim and Frankish.

**Catalan.** The most Iberian box. From its creation (2010) it included the Conquest of Majorca (1229) and the Pisan-Catalan expedition to the Balearics (1113–15). In August 2026 one editor relabeled the Crown of Aragon's wars as crusades: "Croada d'Al-Mayūrqa", "Croada pisano-catalana", "Croada de Barbastre", "Croada d'Almeria (1309)", the conquest of Tortosa. On 17 July 2018 a single edit added a run of Spanish Civil War bombings and offensives (Guernica, Barcelona, Granollers, Xàtiva, the Aragon offensive), removed the same day; probably the Francoist "Cruzada", but the edit has no summary.

**Czech.** Numbers its own crusades: "První, Druhá, Třetí, Čtvrtá křížová výprava proti husitům" (1420, 1421–22, 1427, 1431). Also the Bohemian-Hungarian war of 1468–78 (the crusade against George of Poděbrady) and the 1340 crusade against the Waldensians in Bohemia. The largest heretic section anywhere (11 entries).

**Danish.** Began in 2009 with the Albigensian crusade and the Teutonic Order, not the Levant; Wendish, Swedish, Livonian Order and Sword Brothers followed in 2010. Numbered crusades arrived in 2013, written as digits ("3. korstog"). No Danish crusade to Estonia.

**Dutch.** The Low Countries' own crusades: Stedinger (2019), Drenther (2020, Drenthe is Dutch), "Friese deelname aan de Kruistochten" (2020–21), and the Norman conquest of Sicily as a crusade "tegen de moslims in Sicilië".

**Finnish.** See above: the 2005 crusades to Finland, Häme and Karelia removed in 2007.

**French.** Two boxes. "Modèle:Croisades" (2007) widened in December 2014. In 2025 it added the 1240 campaign against Izborsk and Pskov, labeled "Russie": a crusade against Russia. "Palette Croisades" (2013) is mostly battles (46) and sieges (32) and is the only box with the siege of Lisbon. French and Italian added Henry VI's crusade only in 2020.

**Greek.** Small and stable (23 links). Aragonese crusade 2007, Barbastro 2019, Hussites 2026. Nothing specific to Byzantium beyond the Fourth.

**Hebrew.** 20 links. Besides the nine, a section "מסעי הצלב האחרים" (the other crusades): People's, Children's, Albigensian, Livonian, Prussian, Northern. No crusade after 1291 at all: no Nicopolis, Varna or Alexandria. The Shepherds' Crusade of 1251 was added in 2025; the 1320 Shepherds' Crusade, which attacked Jewish communities, is not in the box.

**Hungarian.** 2019. First to Eighth, 1101, Norwegian, Wendish, Children's, Albigensian, Alexandria and "Nikápolyi hadjárat". No Varna, Hungary's own crusade of 1443–44.

**Italian.** Broad early: the 2008 box already had the northern crusades, Hussites, popular crusades and Otranto. Reconquista only in 2022.

**Norwegian.** Northern crusades since 2005; "Det norske korstog i 1107" (2012); Henry VI's crusade as "Det tyske korstog i 1197" (2019).

**Persian.** See above. Created March 2016 with the Reconquista as "بازپس‌گیری آندلس" (the retaking of Andalus). 83 links, one of the largest boxes.

**Polish.** Numbered I to VII and battles. No northern crusade at any time (no Prussian, Livonian or Lithuanian crusade, no Teutonic Order), and no heretic crusade. Damietta and Alexandria in and out 2007–15.

**Portuguese.** 2005, one of the oldest. Reconquista section 2018 with Barbastro, Majorca and Las Navas; no Lisbon. In 2023 added "Conflitos Luso-Turcos" (Ottoman-Portuguese wars) as a later crusade.

**Russian.** 2018. "Northern Crusades" as one link; no Livonian crusade, no Battle on the Ice, no Pskov. The Levant side is detailed (Thierry of Alsace, the 1129 crusade on Damascus, Barbary).

**Spanish.** See above. The October 2023 restructuring copied the English layout, including the Holy Leagues and the Swedish crusades as "1150", "1249", "1293".

**Swedish.** 2009: First to Third, Children's, "Nordiska korståg", "De svenska korstågen", Reconquista. Fourth to Eighth added in 2013 as links that all go to the general article.

**Turkish.** Two boxes. The first (July 2008) listed Lepanto, the Siege of Vienna of 1683 ("II. Viyana Kuşatması"), the Austro-Turkish wars and Otranto as crusades; removed January 2010. The second (2015) lists battles 1096–1303.

**Ukrainian.** 2011. Northern crusades in detail (Wendish as "Polabian", Livonian, Prussian, Lithuanian, the 1293 Swedish crusade); Reconquista 2019.

## Patterns so far

- National crusades enter national boxes: Hussite crusades in Czech, Drenthe and Stedingen in Dutch, the Balearics and Majorca in Catalan, Ottoman wars in Portuguese and (briefly) Turkish, Baltic orders in Danish.
- Some editions leave out their own: Polish has no Teutonic crusades, Russian no Pskov or Battle on the Ice, Hungarian no Varna, Finnish removed the crusades to Finland, Portuguese has no Lisbon.
- English is the main exporter. The Spanish (2023), Arabic (2017, 2024), Persian and Ukrainian expansions copy its sections and even its labels ("1150", "1249", "1293").
- Outside the canonical series, the only numbers written in a box's own labels are Czech's four crusades against the Hussites. The Swedish crusades are numbered in article titles (English "First Swedish Crusade", Arabic "الحملة الصليبية الأولى على فنلندا") but shown in the boxes by year.

## Method

Templates found through interlanguage links from the English, German, French, Spanish and Italian boxes and from the templates on each main crusade article. Every revision pulled through the MediaWiki API (9 November 2026); links extracted with labels and section headings; blanking vandalism dropped; gaps under three days merged; links resolved to Wikidata items, then sorted into categories by English label and item type, with about 60 items assigned by hand. Battle-only boxes (Arabic, Greek, Persian, Russian, Turkish "battles of the crusades") are left out except where they are the language's main box. Section headings in Portuguese and Arabic were checked by hand after a parsing error.
