# The first English "Crusades" article and 9/11

9 November 2026. Every revision of English Wikipedia's "Crusades", October 2001 to December 2008 (MediaWiki API), plus the 1 July revision of each year 2002–2012.

## The oldest surviving version (rev 349190657, 28 October 2001, Alan Millar)

- About 4,200 bytes, no sources. Its edit summary ("Move pope Innocent III to Fourth Crusade") shows earlier versions existed; they are lost.
- Counts eight: First 1095–1101, Second 1145–47, Third 1188–92, Fourth 1204, Fifth 1217 (Andrew II of Hungary), Sixth 1228–29, Seventh 1249–52, Eighth 1270. No Ninth.
- Children's Crusade (1212) listed after the eight: "Not really a crusade in the same sense as the others listed above."
- "General links" ends with "Alphonso VIII of Spain ''which crusade?''".
- Section "Reputation and evaluation": Islamic fundamentalists use "crusade" "to refer to Western moves against them", and an "interesting symmetry between the terms 'Crusade' and 'Jihad'". Saved 47 days after 11 September; whether written before or after cannot be known.

## Two counts on one page

19 November 2001 (rev 349190658), Millar pasted the *New Schaff-Herzog* article "to integrate". Its contents: "The Fifth, Sixth, and Seventh Crusades, 1228-70 (§ 4)", i.e. the German seven (no Damietta; Frederick II Fifth). Present until 4 April 2002. For four and a half months Frederick II was the Sixth at the top of the page and the Fifth at the bottom.

## What happened to the 9/11-era language

- Jihad "symmetry" paragraph: gone with the 27 April 2004 revision.
- Fundamentalists sentence: present in July 2002, 2003, 2004 samples (2004 reworded: onslaughts by "the Franks" on "cultured Islam"); absent by July 2005.
- George W. Bush's "crusade": only in the July 2008 sample ("in 2002 described his anti-terrorism campaign as a 'crusade'"; he said it on 16 September 2001); absent 2007 and 2009.
- No July sample 2002–2012 mentions September 11, 9/11 or the war on terror.

`data/wikipedia/scope/en_crusades_edits_monthly.csv`. Never more than 9 edits a month from October 2001 to February 2004; no rise after September 2001. Growth from 2005, peak 394 in March 2007, with autumn and spring highs and summer lows.
