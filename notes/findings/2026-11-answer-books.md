# The exam answer books, 1826–36

28 November 2026. Direction D1: did the baccalauréat answer books copy Desmichels's numbering? Browser session, Google Books text layer, with one page image. Four rows added to `data/works.csv`, one citation, one reason, four search-log rows. This is the first session script under the reconciled model: `scripts/add_answer_books_2026_11_28.py`.

## Result

Yes, and one of them says so. Bouchitté, Charma, Saigey and Lesieur's *Manuel des aspirants au baccalauréat ès-lettres* (Paris: L. Hachette, 1836) answers question XIV, "Précis historique des croisades," under numbered headings, "2o croisade" through "7o croisade," ending with "8e et dernière croisade." The slots are the modern eight: 1202–04 fourth, the Hungarian-led Lateran crusade fifth, Frederick II sixth, Louis IX seventh, Tunis eighth. The crusade preached under Celestine III and "interrompue à la mort de Henri VI" is mentioned at the head of the fourth, without a number. A footnote (second pagination, p. 158, page image viewed) gives the source:

> Nous avons adopté la division des croisades indiquée dans le Précis d'histoire du moyen-âge de M. Desmichels.

This is the first explicit attribution of the modern eight to a source anywhere in the project. **Firm.**

## The six answer books read

| Book | Year | Q. XIV numbering | Row |
|---|---|---|---|
| *Manuel complet des aspirans* (Delalain) | 1824 | Desmichels's numbered list, copied | `sb-1824-manuel-aspirans-bac` |
| Ponelle, *Nouveau manuel complet* | 1826 | None; "quatrième croisade" in passing | `sb-1826-ponelle-nouveau-manuel` |
| Delavigne, *Manuel complet* | 1829 | "Les cinquième et sixième croisades" (1217–1228); "les deux dernières" for Louis IX | `sb-1829-delavigne-manuel` |
| Ponelle, *Manuel complet* (Mansut fils) | 1829 | None; as in 1826 | `sb-1829-ponelle-manuel` |
| Delavigne, *Manuel complet* | 1835 | As in 1829, now naming Andrew of Hungary for the fifth and Frederick for the sixth | `sb-1835-delavigne-manuel` |
| Bouchitté et al. (Hachette) | 1836 | Full modern eight under numbered headings; cites Desmichels | `sb-1836-bouchitte-manuel` |

Four of the six use the modern slots and two number nothing. None uses the old dictionary count (Henry VI fourth) or Koch's seven. Delavigne writes his own prose and states only the fifth and sixth; the fourth, seventh and eighth follow from his sequence but are not stated, so his rows carry D 5 and F 6 only. That pair alone separates the modern eight from every rival family.

## What this does to the argument

1. **D1 answered.** The examination asked for the crusades without numbering them (Hubert 1826). The answer books supplied the numbers, and the numbers they supplied were Desmichels's, from 1824 to 1836. The diffusion ran through the exam-preparation market, as proposed on 27 November.
2. **The agent is documented, not just inferred.** Until now the link from Desmichels to later books was shared wording and dates. Bouchitté names him.
3. **One publisher (inference, flagged).** Bouchitté's manual is a Hachette book. Desmichels's *Précis* was on Hachette's list by 1834 (see the gaps pass), and Bouillet's *Dictionnaire* of 1842, until November the project's earliest witness, is Hachette too. The modern eight was in Hachette's school list eight years before Bouillet printed it. Whether Bouillet took it from his publisher's own textbooks is untested, but the path is now short.
4. **A loose end.** Saigey, one of the four authors, is probably J.-F. Saigey, whose *Traité de métrologie* (Hachette, 1834) has Koch's seven, not the eight. If so, the same team carried both counts within two years. Identity not checked.
